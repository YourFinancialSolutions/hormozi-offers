## 4. Attribution and corrections

Do not silently convert book rhetoric, illustrative arithmetic, historical reports, or recommendations into established universal facts.

| Location | Source issue or distinction | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| PDF 36–37 | Customers acquired per month × lifetime value mixes an acquisition rate with lifetime customer economics | Calculate **expected lifetime gross profit of the monthly acquisition cohort**. It is not profit or cash recognized in that month. Revenue-based CLV also differs from this book's gross-profit LTV |
| PDF 40–43 | Table uses rounded appointments, close rates, prices and totals; prose says 2.5 × 2.5 × 4 = 22.4 | That product is 25. Table's reported totals yield 112,000 / 5,000 = 22.4. Exact 28 × 3,997 = 111,916. Explain rounding/inconsistency; compare cash, not net profit. See E1 |
| PDF 53, 67–68 | Broadening thresholds and business results are author's contextual guidance/reports | Treat as historical heuristics and reported results; no promise or universal cutoff. Event survey is not controlled evidence |
| PDF 75 | Division by zero and “infinite value” language | Use a directional model; division by zero is undefined. Do not plug subjective scores into a monetary valuation formula |
| PDF 83 | One sentence groups high perceived likelihood with undesirable high effort/delay | Follow the diagram and rest of chapter: likelihood goes **up**, delay and effort go **down** |
| PDF 125–128, 141 | Nonlinear willingness-to-pay and late-launch sales shares | Author heuristics/examples; test actual demand. Do not assume 20% will pay 5× or a fixed share will buy at deadline |
| PDF 142 | Seasonal example includes February 30 | Use a real calendar date; do not reuse the typo |
| PDF 148–153 | “Always assign value” and highlight bundled components as bonuses | Preserve author view in explanation; added rule: substantiate monetary anchors or omit them. Highlighting must not make essential promised support unavailable |
| PDF 156 | 117 retained sales vs 95 described as more money flowing to bottom line | Retained-sales ratio is 1.2316; profit requires fulfillment, claims, acquisition and overhead costs |
| PDF 162–164 | Continuing service presented as avoiding refund exposure | It preserves collected cash but still costs labor/capacity and can create open-ended liability |
| PDF 164–166 | Wage remedy mentions 1,960 annual hours, discouraging claims; anti-guarantee passage suggests inventing a reason | 40 × 52 = 2,080; 1,960 implies 49 working weeks. Agree the basis and simple evidence. Use a truthful reason and a usable claim process |
| PDF 171 | $20 per thousand × 200,000 people described as $10,000 | Simple product is $4,000; neither number establishes actual unique reach or current media costs |
| PDF 174–175 | Some unquantified goal/time names treated as easier to advertise | Removing a number does not remove an implied outcome claim. Verify current rules only when a real campaign requires it |
| PDF 178, 187–188 | Future volumes have provisional names/numbering | Do not use these pages as a current bibliography or claim all later-book frameworks are included |

## 5. How to extend the knowledge base

When adding source material, record its document/edition and page convention. Update the chapter row and concept lookup. Add the material to its existing concept home before creating another file. Keep examples, procedures, and interpretations labeled. If a new edition differs, retain both locations rather than overwriting old references with guessed offsets.

When asked for coverage, report concepts covered, example depth, and explicit omissions. File counts or word counts alone are not proof of completeness.
