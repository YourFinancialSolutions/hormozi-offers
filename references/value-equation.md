# Value Equation

Use the Value Equation to engineer real and perceived value:

`Perceived value rises as desired outcome and belief in achievement rise, and as perceived delay and effort/sacrifice fall.`

Treat the relationship as directional, not as a precise financial formula.

## 1. Desired outcome

Define the destination in the buyer's language, not the service vehicle.

Ask:

- What does the buyer ultimately want to experience, possess, avoid, or become?
- What changes financially, functionally, emotionally, socially, or in identity?
- How would people important to the buyer notice the result?
- What deeper status, security, freedom, belonging, or control motive sits beneath the stated goal?
- Which outcome matters most now?

Write:

`The buyer wants [specific outcome] so they can [deeper benefit or identity change], without [major feared tradeoff].`

Do not inflate the promise. Make it concrete enough to recognize but broad enough to remain truthful across qualified buyers.

## 2. Perceived likelihood of achievement

Increase rational confidence that this offer can work for this buyer.

Audit:

- Relevant track record and representative case evidence.
- Demonstration, sample, diagnostic, or proof of process.
- Clear mechanism and milestones.
- Qualification criteria and honest exclusions.
- Credentials or third-party validation when relevant.
- Customer responsibilities and support.
- Guarantee or performance alignment.
- Reduction of technical, operational, and adoption risk.

Distinguish:

- **Actual probability:** how often the delivery system produces the result for qualified customers.
- **Perceived probability:** what the buyer reasonably believes from the evidence and explanation.

Improve both. Never use selective proof to manufacture certainty.

## 3. Time delay

Map two timelines:

- **Time to first value:** the first meaningful sign that the purchase was correct.
- **Time to full outcome:** the expected path to the dream result.

Create a fast-win ladder:

| Moment | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|
| Immediately after purchase | Access, assessment, plan, setup, confirmation, or removal of uncertainty. |
| First 24-72 hours | First completed action, visible artifact, quick fix, or baseline measurement. |
| First week | Early result or milestone tied to the final outcome. |
| Ongoing | Progress reporting, checkpoints, and recovery plan when off track. |

Shorten actual delay where feasible. When physical or regulatory reality prevents it, reduce uncertainty through transparent milestones and status visibility.

## 4. Effort and sacrifice

Map every customer cost beyond price:

- Time and scheduling.
- Number of decisions and steps.
- Learning and technical skill.
- Data, documents, approvals, or access required.
- Physical, emotional, social, or reputational discomfort.
- Switching cost and fear of disruption.
- Opportunity cost.
- Family, staff, or partner coordination.
- Ongoing discipline and maintenance.

Reduce effort through done-for-you work, guided implementation, defaults, automation, templates, checklists, pre-built assets, concierge onboarding, clear ownership, reminders, and fewer handoffs.

Do not hide unavoidable effort. State it and design support around it.

## 5. Perception and proof

Value exists commercially only when the buyer can perceive it. Communicate:

- What changes.
- Why the mechanism creates the change.
- What happens first and next.
- What the buyer must do.
- How progress is recognized.
- What evidence supports the belief.

Look for both logical and psychological friction. A process may be objectively fast yet feel slow because progress is invisible. A process may be simple yet feel difficult because ownership is unclear.

Use psychological solutions only when truthful: visibility, progress markers, orientation, defaults, social support, confidence-building, and reduced uncertainty. Do not disguise poor delivery.

## 6. Value audit

Score each lever 1-10 with evidence:

| Lever | Score question |
|---|---|
| Outcome appeal | How strongly does the qualified buyer want this result now? |
| Belief | How certain are they that this offer can work for them? |
| Speed | How quickly do they receive meaningful value and the full result? |
| Ease | How little effort, sacrifice, risk, and disruption are required? |

For speed and ease, 10 means faster/easier. Identify:

1. Current evidence.
2. Main friction.
3. Actual delivery improvement.
4. Communication improvement.
5. Metric to validate the change.

Prioritize the weakest lever that can be improved without compromising delivery. When headline outcomes are similar across competitors, focus on belief, time to first value, and effort reduction.

## Value Equation deliverable

Provide:

- One-sentence dream outcome.
- Four-lever scorecard.
- Fast-win ladder.
- Proof plan.
- Customer-effort map.
- Three highest-impact value improvements ranked by impact, cost, and confidence.
