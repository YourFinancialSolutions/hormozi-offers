# Market, Positioning, and Premium Pricing

Use this reference before building deliverables. A strong offer cannot reliably rescue a structurally bad market.

## Define the offer and growth objective

Keep these distinct:

- **Product or service:** what the business makes or does.
- **Offer:** the product or service, price, payment terms, scope, conditions, promotion, risk reversal, and buying terms presented for exchange.
- **Promotion:** the temporary reason or wrapper used to stimulate response.
- **Sales message:** how the offer is explained.

Growth ultimately comes from more customers and/or more value per customer. For offer work, track:

`Monthly gross profit from new customers = new customers × lifetime gross profit per customer`

Use gross profit rather than revenue when comparing acquisition capacity. Do not invent retention, margin, or acquisition figures.

## Run the market gate

Assess four indicators with evidence, not enthusiasm.

| Indicator | Questions | Strong evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | Is the problem urgent, costly, frequent, emotional, or identity-relevant? What happens if it remains unsolved? | Buyers already spend time or money trying to solve it. |
| Purchasing power | Can this buyer personally authorize and afford the required price? Is budget accessible now? | Existing category spend, income, budget, financing capacity, or clear ROI. |
| Reachability | Can the segment be identified and reached repeatedly? | Lists, associations, search behavior, communities, job titles, geography, or channel concentration. |
| Market direction | Is the segment stable or growing rather than structurally shrinking? | Customer counts, category demand, regulation, demographic, or technology trend. |

Score each 1-10 and cite the basis. Treat the score as a decision aid, not scientific measurement.

- A pain or purchasing-power score below 6 is a major warning.
- A reachability score below 5 means the offer may be difficult to distribute even if it is good.
- A market-direction score below 4 requires a compelling strategic reason to continue.
- A normal, stable market can work. Do not require a temporary frenzy.

Use this priority when diagnosing failure:

`Market quality > offer strength > persuasion skill`

Do not use better copy to conceal weak demand.

## Select a commercially useful niche

Define the niche using:

`Specific buyer + specific painful situation + specific desired result + relevant context`

Add a distinct mechanism only when it is real and useful.

Create a specificity ladder:

1. Broad category.
2. Buyer type.
3. Buyer plus problem.
4. Buyer plus problem plus context.
5. Buyer plus problem plus outcome and mechanism.

Choose the narrowest level that still has enough reachable buyers and purchasing power. Avoid both "everyone" and a segment too small to sustain the business.

Once a viable niche is selected, keep the buyer stable long enough to test multiple offers. Do not confuse one failed promotion with a failed market. Broaden only when capacity, total addressable market, or evidence justifies it.

## Diagnose commoditization

Ask:

- What alternatives will the buyer compare side by side?
- Do competitors describe the same work with the same scope and pricing model?
- Is the decision currently reduced to hourly rate, monthly fee, or unit price?
- Does the prospect ask "why are you more expensive?" before understanding the outcome?
- Could a competitor copy the headline while leaving the offer essentially unchanged?

Create a category-of-one thesis:

`For [specific buyer] facing [specific painful situation], provide [desired outcome] through [credible mechanism and delivery model], with [meaningful proof or risk reversal], unlike [ordinary category alternative].`

A differentiated offer should improve response, conversion, price, or all three. It must still deliver materially different value; naming alone is not differentiation.

## Establish premium-pricing logic

Build a large, defensible gap between perceived value and price by increasing value before choosing the final price.

Use this sequence:

1. Estimate the economic, emotional, time, risk, and status value of the desired outcome.
2. Identify the buyer's current cost of delay and current alternative spend.
3. Build the Value Equation and package.
4. Calculate the delivery floor and target gross profit.
5. Select a price that funds excellent delivery and still leaves the buyer a compelling value surplus.
6. Test the offer with qualified buyers and actual payment behavior.

Avoid competitor-average pricing as the primary method. Competitors may have weak margins, different proof, or a commoditized offer.

Premium pricing can create a reinforcing cycle when supported by delivery:

`More margin → better people/systems/experience → stronger outcomes/proof → higher buyer confidence → greater pricing power`

It may also increase buyer commitment when buyer action is necessary. Never use a high price as a substitute for value or proof.

## Check economics

At minimum, calculate or mark unknown:

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Price collected | Cash and timing of collection. |
| Direct fulfillment cost | Incremental labor, materials, software, shipping, partner cost. |
| Expected remedy cost | Refunds, free extensions, credits, or guarantee payouts. |
| Gross profit | Price minus direct fulfillment and expected remedy cost. |
| Capacity | Customers deliverable per week or month at promised quality. |
| Allowable acquisition cost | Maximum sustainable cost to acquire a customer. |
| Payback period | Time until acquisition and fulfillment cash is recovered. |

Flag offers that create cash today but an unsustainable delivery liability later.

## Market and pricing deliverable

Provide:

1. Market scorecard with evidence and risks.
2. Recommended avatar and rejected alternatives.
3. Commodity comparison and category-of-one thesis.
4. Price-value case and proposed testing range.
5. Economics table with unknowns clearly marked.
