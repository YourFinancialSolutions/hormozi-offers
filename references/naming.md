# Naming Offers with MAGIC

Use naming to make the right buyer recognize the offer, outcome, timing, and format. Naming is the wrapper, not the core value.

Book basis: chapter 16, PDF pp. 170–180; MAGIC diagram p. 173. Read [knowledge-naming-execution.md](knowledge-naming-execution.md) for explanation, source nuances and examples. ## 1. Build with MAGIC

Combine three to five components. Change the order when it improves clarity.

| Letter | Component | Function | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| M | Magnet | Give a truthful, attention-worthy reason for the promotion. | Why now? Why this promotion? What is unusual or valuable? |
| A | Avatar | Call out the intended buyer. | Who should immediately recognize this is for them? |
| G | Goal | State the desired result or experience. | What does the buyer want? |
| I | Interval | Indicate duration or timing where supportable. | When is the experience, milestone, or completion? |
| C | Container | Signal a structured bundle or method. | Is it a program, sprint, system, assessment, intensive, workshop, or service? |

Keep the name short enough to say and remember. Specificity should not destroy clarity.

### Magnet

Use a real reason: opening, anniversary, seasonal need, limited pilot, new service level, capacity window, partnership, or approved promotion. Never invent a reason.

### Avatar

Use the buyer identity, problem state, profession, life stage, business type, or local area. Use the narrowest description that improves recognition without excluding good buyers accidentally.

### Goal

Name a recognizable outcome, milestone, relief, or identity change. Avoid unsupported claims.

### Interval

Use the program duration, onboarding time, first milestone, event length, or campaign period. Do not pair a quantified result and deadline when it implies a guarantee the business cannot support or advertise legally. Removing a number does not automatically remove an implied outcome claim.

### Container

Choose a word that fits the delivery. Do not call a simple consultation an accelerator or mastermind unless the experience supports it.

## 2. Generate names

Create a word bank for each component, then combine deliberately.

Generate a useful range for the request; for a broad naming exercise, consider:

- Five clear and literal names.
- Five premium or authority-oriented names.
- Five outcome-led names.
- Five local or seasonal names when relevant.

Optionally use natural rhyme or alliteration. Do not force cleverness at the expense of understanding.

## 3. Score and test

Score each name 1-5:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Avatar recognition | Does the intended buyer know it is for them? |
| Outcome relevance | Does it point to something they want now? |
| Clarity | Can a stranger roughly understand the offer? |
| Distinctiveness | Is it separable from commodity alternatives? |
| Memorability | Is it concise, pronounceable, and repeatable? |
| Credibility | Does it sound believable given the proof? |
| Compliance | Are claims and timing supportable? |

Recommend the highest-scoring name and two test alternatives. Actual response data outranks preference.

## 4. Name components and bonuses

Use the same method for the package stack. A component name should communicate:

`Obstacle solved + mechanism or asset + expected benefit`

Do not use a vague branded name without an explanatory subtitle.

## 5. Refresh fatigued offers

Change the lightest variable first:

1. Creative image or video.
2. Hook, story, or body copy.
3. Headline or offer name.
4. Seasonal reason.
5. Duration or start date.
6. Bonus or promotional enhancer.
7. Core packaging or pricing model only when evidence requires it.

Preserve a proven delivery system while testing new wrappers.

## 6. Apply compliance controls

Check:

- Income, savings, weight, health, tax, insurance, credit, and legal outcome claims.
- Quantified result plus time interval.
- "Free," discount, and original-price substantiation.
- Locality and eligibility.
- Trademark or confusingly similar names.
- Platform advertising restrictions.

Generate a compliant alternative when the strongest direct-response name creates claim risk.
