## 1. Interview protocol

Ask no more than five questions per round. Skip questions already answered.

### Round 1: Business and market

1. What do you sell now, to whom, at what price and payment terms?
2. What urgent problem makes buyers seek a solution now?
3. What result do they want, and what alternatives do they compare?
4. How do you identify and reach these buyers?
5. What proof shows the business can deliver?

### Round 2: Customer journey and obstacles

1. What must the customer do before, during, and after purchase?
2. Where do prospects hesitate, fail, quit, or ask repeated questions?
3. What switching, setup, learning, time, or stakeholder burdens exist?
4. What early milestone predicts long-term success?
5. What next problem appears after the primary result?

### Round 3: Delivery and economics

1. What is delivered now, by whom, in what format, and over what timeline?
2. What is the direct cost and labor per customer?
3. What weekly or monthly capacity can be delivered at promised quality?
4. What parts can be standardized, automated, or delivered one-to-many?
5. What gross margin, acquisition cost, and payback constraints matter?

### Round 4: Risk and compliance

1. What result can the business control and measure?
2. What must the customer do to succeed?
3. What refund, cancellation, guarantee, or regulatory rules apply?
4. What claims, testimonials, values, deadlines, and capacity limits can be substantiated?
5. What worst-case liability can the business survive?

Allow the user to answer "unknown." Convert unknowns into validation tasks rather than inventing facts.

## 4. Offer audit score

This is an operational scorecard, not a score published in the book.

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Market and avatar | 20 |
| Value Equation | 20 |
| Problem coverage and delivery design | 20 |
| Differentiation and price-value case | 15 |
| Economics and fulfillment | 10 |
| Enhancements | 10 |
| Clarity, proof, and compliance | 5 |

For each category, provide evidence, lost points, and the next improvement. Interpret:

- 85-100: strong enough for disciplined testing, subject to unresolved facts.
- 70-84: promising but material weaknesses remain.
- 50-69: rebuild before scaling traffic.
- Below 50: foundational market, outcome, or delivery problem.

Do not use the total to hide a fatal flaw. A weak market, impossible promise, negative economics, or unlawful claim overrides the numeric score.

## 5. Final deliverable

For an explanation from the book, answer the requested concept with source pages, mechanism, relevant example, important nuance, and a clearly labeled application if useful. Use [book-examples.md](book-examples.md) and [worked-offers.md](worked-offers.md) as needed. Do not force the full commercial document below onto a learning question.

For a practical deliverable, include a short source/decision rationale when requested or useful. Keep author concepts, implementation additions and hypothetical assumptions distinguishable. Do not put citations or internal methodology labels into customer-facing sales copy.

For a full build, provide sections in this order:

1. **Executive recommendation:** the single recommended offer and why.
2. **Facts and assumptions:** known facts, unknowns, and validation needs.
3. **Market and niche:** scorecard and selected avatar.
4. **Category-of-one thesis:** ordinary comparison versus new offer.
5. **Dream outcome and Value Equation:** scores and improvements.
6. **Problem-solution map:** prioritized obstacles and solutions.
7. **Delivery design:** vehicle decisions and Trim & Stack rationale.
8. **Core offer:** scope, sequence, timeline, responsibilities, exclusions, and tiers.
9. **Pricing:** price, terms, value basis, economics, and testing range.
10. **Bonuses:** names, problems solved, value basis, and delivery.
11. **Guarantee:** type, exact plain-language terms, remedy, conditions, and risks.
12. **Scarcity and urgency:** real limits, dates, evidence, and enforcement.
13. **Names:** recommended name and alternatives scored with MAGIC.
14. **Sales assets:** one-sentence offer, headline, short pitch, CTA, objection responses, and FAQ.
15. **Validation plan:** test, metrics, success threshold, stop threshold, and next decision.

For an audit, lead with the score, fatal flaws, and the three highest-leverage changes.

## 6. Validation plan

Use the smallest test that produces real buying evidence:

1. Define one segment, one offer, one price, and one conversion event.
2. Present it to qualified prospects through an existing trusted channel when possible.
3. Track exposure, response, qualification, purchase, refund/remedy, fulfillment effort, and gross profit.
4. Record the exact reason for every no-sale and every delivery exception.
5. Change one major variable at a time where feasible.

Set in advance:

- Minimum number of qualified conversations or purchases.
- Success threshold.
- Stop or revision threshold.
- Maximum delivery capacity.
- Maximum acceptable acquisition and remedy cost.
- Test duration.

When an offer has traction, vary creative, copy, and wrapper before rebuilding operations. Use `$hormozi-leads` when the user is ready to scale acquisition.
