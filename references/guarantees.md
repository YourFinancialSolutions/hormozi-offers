# Guarantees and Risk Reversal

Use guarantees to reduce the buyer's risk without creating an unsustainable liability. Spend substantial design effort here; risk often remains the final objection after value is established.

## 1. Map buyer risk

Ask what the buyer fears losing:

- Purchase price.
- Time.
- Implementation effort.
- Switching or disruption cost.
- Advertising, travel, materials, or other ancillary cost.
- Reputation or internal credibility.
- Opportunity cost.
- Contractual commitment.
- Privacy, security, or compliance exposure.
- Failure to achieve the desired result.

Rank the risks by decision impact. Reverse the largest risk the business can credibly assume.

## 2. Choose a guarantee family

### Unconditional

Allow a refund or cancellation within a defined period without performance conditions.

Use when:

- The product is low-cost or reversible.
- Fulfillment cost is low.
- Satisfaction is easy to assess.
- Abuse risk is manageable.

### Conditional

Provide a remedy when the buyer completes specified success-driving actions and the defined result or service standard is not achieved.

Use when:

- Buyer actions materially affect results.
- Those actions can be tracked.
- The result and period can be measured.
- The remedy is financially survivable.

Conditions should guide customers toward success, not create a hidden maze that makes claims impossible.

### Anti-guarantee

State that sales are final or no outcome guarantee is provided, with a truthful and prominent reason.

Use when:

- The value is consumed, copied, customized, or irreversible.
- Fulfillment cost is high.
- Outcome is primarily outside the seller's control.
- Regulation or professional standards limit guarantees.

An anti-guarantee is a risk position, not permission to conceal poor quality. The reason must be real.

### Implied or performance-aligned

Tie some or all compensation to a measurable result, revenue share, profit share, event, or performance trigger.

Use when:

- Outcomes and attribution are transparent.
- Data access and payment collection are reliable.
- Both parties understand baseline, deductions, timing, and audit rights.
- The business can absorb delayed or variable payment.

Consider a minimum fee plus performance compensation when needed to cover delivery cost.

## 3. Design the terms

Use this structure:

`If [qualified customer] completes [specific controllable actions] and does not receive [measurable result or service standard] by [time or event], the business will provide [specific remedy] through [simple claim process].`

Define:

- Eligibility.
- Customer actions.
- Business actions.
- Measurement source.
- Start and end points.
- Remedy.
- Claim method and response time.
- Exclusions and extraordinary circumstances.
- Maximum liability when appropriate.

Write the plain-language version first, then obtain legal review where warranted.

## 4. Select a remedy

Possible remedies include:

- Full or partial refund.
- Refund of specified ancillary cost.
- Continue working until the defined result.
- Additional fixed service period.
- Credit toward another service.
- Personal or senior-expert intervention.
- Release from a commitment.
- Delay the next payment until an activation milestone.
- Cover specified operating cost until the first outcome.
- Pay only when performance occurs.

Choose the remedy that directly reverses the buyer's main fear. Do not offer a larger remedy merely for drama.

## 5. Evaluate the economics

Compare current and proposed scenarios:

`Expected retained customers = purchases × (1 - refund or remedy rate)`

`Expected contribution = collected revenue - direct fulfillment cost - acquisition cost - expected refund/remedy cost`

Model:

- Expected conversion lift.
- Expected claim rate.
- Cost per claim.
- Fraud or abuse.
- Cash timing.
- Maximum simultaneous liability.
- Operational capacity for extensions or personal service.

High-cost services usually require conditional, service, credit, release, or anti-guarantee structures rather than broad unconditional refunds.

## 6. Stack guarantees

Stack only when each layer reverses a different risk or applies to a different period.

Examples of structures:

- Short unconditional inspection period plus a longer conditional result guarantee.
- Activation milestone guarantee plus longer-term service guarantee.
- Service-level guarantee plus performance-based fee component.

Avoid stacks that contradict each other or make administration unclear.

## 7. Apply compliance controls

Never:

- Guarantee outcomes the business cannot control.
- Hide conditions or make the claim process unreasonably difficult.
- Call ordinary cancellation rights a special guarantee.
- Use an anti-guarantee with a fabricated justification.
- Ignore required refund, cooling-off, professional, insurance, financial, health, tax, or consumer-protection rules.

Distinguish a satisfaction guarantee, service-level guarantee, and outcome guarantee. Use representative proof and suitable disclosures.

## Guarantee decision scorecard

Score 1-5:

| Dimension | High score means |
|---|---|
| Controllability | The business and required customer actions strongly influence the result. |
| Measurability | Result, timeline, and evidence are objective. |
| Affordability | Worst-case remedy is survivable. |
| Administrability | Eligibility and claims are easy to verify and fulfill. |
| Buyer impact | It reverses a major purchase fear. |
| Compliance confidence | Terms fit industry and jurisdiction requirements. |

Recommend one primary guarantee, one conservative alternative, and the reason for rejecting unsuitable types.
