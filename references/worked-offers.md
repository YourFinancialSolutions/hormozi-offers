# Practice: Complete Original Offer Applications

All cases in this file are **Adaptations** created for the skill. They are not in the book, verified user-business facts, live offers, or financial forecasts. Every price, volume, cost, timeline and metric is hypothetical. Use them to learn decision structure; replace assumptions before recommending a real offer.

The method comes from the concepts indexed in [book-map.md](book-map.md). The worksheets, classifications, calculations and test gates are **Implementation** tools. Procedures: [interview-output.md](interview-output.md).

## 1. Bookkeeping: complete construction

### Facts supplied for the hypothetical exercise

A small bookkeeping team serves owner-operated service businesses. It wants an entry package for up to three months of historical bookkeeping, two accounts, and 300 total transactions. Illustrative labor is six hours at $40/hour, plus $20 direct tools/processing. It has 30 hours per month available for this pilot. There are no verified savings testimonials or market demand data in the exercise.

**Assumption ledger:** The team can deliver the scope in six hours; buyers value organized historical records; records arrive complete; $900 is an acceptable trial price; acquisition costs $100 per sale; an expected $20 per sale reserve is sufficient for incremental remedies. Each needs validation. A high confidence score would be unjustified.

### Market and avatar

Proposed buyer: an owner-operated service business with recent bookkeeping backlog, enough revenue to pay for help, and an upcoming accountant review. Pain and purchasing power are plausible but unverified. Targetability could come from existing professional relationships; no referral agreement or permission to contact clients is assumed.

Choose this over “all small businesses” because the backlog scope and buying trigger make delivery and communication more repeatable. Do not claim tax savings, audit protection or government acceptance as the outcome.

### Dream outcome and differentiation

The buyer wants recent records organized for accountant review, with a clear list of unresolved items and less manual document chasing.

Old comparison: hourly data entry or a generic monthly bookkeeping fee. Proposed comparison: a scoped cleanup with input checklist, reconciliation, exception handling, review package, a handoff call and defined rework terms. The differentiator is the managed path to usable records, not a new name for data entry.

### Four-lever design

| Lever | Likely friction | Design response | What to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Buyer cannot visualize what “clean books” means | Show an anonymized/sample handoff package | Buyer recognizes its usefulness; sample clearly labeled |
| Likelihood | Buyer fears their records are too messy | Preflight scope review and visible exception list | Which missing-data cases exceed the standard scope |
| Time | No clear start or finish | Confirm inputs within two business days; target delivery within ten business days after complete inputs | Team can meet these proposed service times |
| Effort | Receipts spread across email and portals | One collection checklist and guided intake | Actual customer time and recurring missing inputs |

The two-day milestone is a completeness assessment and plan, not finished bookkeeping. The ten-day clock starts only when required inputs are complete, and this must be clear before purchase.

### Problem-to-solution map

| Journey | Obstacle | Solution requirement | Selected vehicle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before purchase | Unsure whether backlog fits | Know scope and exceptions first | Short preflight with written scope |
| Intake | Documents scattered | Collect in one guided sequence | Checklist plus upload instructions |
| Setup | Doesn't know account export steps | Export correctly without learning accounting | Brief illustrated guides and limited help |
| Processing | Ambiguous transactions | Resolve questions without repeated disruption | Consolidated question list |
| Review | Doesn't know what is still missing | See completed work and unresolved exceptions | Reconciliation/exception summary |
| Handoff | Accountant asks follow-up questions | Understand package and responsibility boundaries | One scheduled handoff call |
| After completion | Backlog begins again | Maintain a simple monthly routine | Optional highlighted maintenance checklist |

### Generate, then trim vehicles

| Candidate | Buyer value / cost | Decision and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Founder personally retrieves every receipt | High convenience / very high and uncertain labor | Reject for standard scope; customer provides documents |
| Large accounting training course | Low relevance / high creation and consumption effort | Remove; buyer wants work completed |
| Consolidated document checklist | High / low after creation | Keep; reduces avoidable missing inputs |
| Illustrated account-export guides | High for setup / low per reuse | Keep with maintenance owner |
| Daily unlimited calls | Uncertain incremental value / high capacity burden | Replace with one handoff call and defined question handling |
| Complete transaction cleanup | Essential / high direct cost | Keep; price and scope must fund it |

Do not remove reconciliation to reduce cost while still promising reconciled records. Do not promise to reconstruct documents that do not exist. Refer out-of-scope periods/accounts for a separate quote.

### Final stack and price

Proposed name: **“Service Business Books Reset”**, with a subtitle specifying three months, account/transaction limits and the handoff deliverable. It uses avatar, goal and container; no need to force all MAGIC elements into the title.

Core: preflight, scoped cleanup, reconciliation summary, unresolved-items list and handoff call. Support: intake checklist and export guidance. Highlighted bonus: a reusable monthly maintenance checklist; no fabricated standalone price. More extensive ongoing bookkeeping is a possible later offer, not automatically bundled.

Illustrative price: $900 collected at acceptance of scope. The value case is saved reconstruction effort and clearer records; a dollar claim about savings remains unproven. A 50/50 payment option changes cash timing, not automatically value or profit.

| Economics per pilot sale | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Price | $900 |
| Direct labor: 6 × $40 | $240 |
| Other direct cost | $20 |
| Expected incremental remedy allowance | $20 |
| Contribution before acquisition/overhead | $620 |
| Assumed acquisition cost | $100 |
| Contribution after acquisition, before overhead/tax | $520 |

At eight hours instead of six, direct labor is $320 and contribution becomes $440. At twelve hours, labor is $480 and contribution becomes $280. This shows why scope and time measurement matter more than adding an inflated bonus value.

With 30 available hours, begin with three pilot clients: 18 planned hours plus room for exceptions and learning. The three-client cap is a proposed operational decision, not evidence that three places are nearly sold out. Pre-creation time for assets and overhead still need separate budgeting.

### Guarantee and availability

Prefer a defined service-correction promise: if the delivered work does not match the agreed checklist because of the team's error, correct it without additional fees within an agreed service period. The final terms need an objective standard, simple claim route, period and responsibilities. Do not guarantee audit results, tax savings or that no future accountant questions arise.

A possible remedy for missing a seller-controlled delivery deadline is a fixed fee credit, but model it before adding it. An unlimited refund-plus-unlimited-rework stack is unnecessarily costly for this exercise. Do not call a finite correction policy the book's unlimited service-until-outcome guarantee.

Use a real pilot intake cap only after the owner adopts it. There is no seasonal deadline in the supplied facts, so omit one. State the next available start honestly.

### Buyer-facing explanation

“Get up to three months of your business records organized for accountant review. We guide document collection, reconcile the agreed accounts, flag unresolved items and walk you through the handoff. The fixed scope and timeline are confirmed before you pay.”

This is a practice draft. Price, time and proof would be added only after verification. “I lack receipts” is answered through the completeness check and exceptions process, not a promise that missing receipts no longer matter.

### Validation and next decision

Use a small existing source of qualified conversations if authorized. Proposed pilot: up to three paid deliveries, with a review after ten qualified conversations or the pilot capacity is filled. These numbers are a learning budget, not statistically validated thresholds.

Track purchase decisions, exact objections, document-completeness delays, labor, rework and whether the receiving accountant can use the package. Predefine revision triggers: repeated scope overruns, delivery exceeding eight hours, unclear exceptions, or failures to meet the promised checklist. If conversations show interest but no purchases, inspect trust, price and fit; do not label the whole market invalid from ten responses. Before scaling, verify positive contribution and repeatable quality.

## 2. Expense software: a different delivery model

### Hypothetical input and market

Assume an expense app for owner-operator truck drivers already supports receipt photos, voice entry, categorization review and report export. These are exercise assumptions, **not verified TruckBooks capabilities**. Illustrative subscription is $25/month; direct monthly support/hosting is $5; retention assumption is eight months; acquisition cost assumption is $60. The team can offer four guided setup appointments this month.

The buyer wants less after-hours reconstruction of expenses and a report they can hand to their accountant. Do not promise deductible status, tax savings, or an audit outcome. A market interview must confirm whether the main barrier is capturing expenses, categorizing them correctly, or remembering to use the app.

### Design the offer through the four levers

- Outcome: a repeatable capture-and-review routine ending in an exportable report.
- Likelihood: show a real import/capture/review/export demonstration with sample data; label samples.
- Time: help the buyer capture one expense and inspect its report placement in the first session. This is activation, not a finished annual report.
- Effort: short capture path, review prompts and plain instructions. The user still checks uncertain categories and provides accurate records.

| Obstacle | Candidate vehicles | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| “I won't set it up” | Video tour, interactive defaults, founder setup call | Defaults plus a short guide for everyone; limited optional guided setup |
| “I have an old pile of receipts” | Automated supported import, manual entry, human cleanup | State supported import limits; separately price human cleanup if offered |
| “I won't trust the report” | Sample export, live accountant testimonial, review checklist | Demonstrate actual output and provide review checklist; no invented testimonial |
| “I forget on the road” | Gentle reminders, automatic entry without review, weekly routine | Use a configurable routine; do not assume automation can resolve all missing facts |

Trim a proposed 40-video “accounting academy”: it adds work to an ease-focused offer. Keep concise setup guidance. Do not sell unlimited human bookkeeping at a $25 software price without changing economics and capabilities.

### Package and terms

Core: supported software features, ordinary setup guide and report export. Support: first-record walkthrough and category review prompts. Bonus candidate: a short accountant-handoff checklist without an invented monetary anchor. Optional service tier: guided setup, genuinely capped at four appointments if adopted.

Possible name: **“Owner-Operator Expense Routine”** plus a subtitle describing capture, review and export. This intentionally avoids a quantified tax result and artificial time promise.

Unlimited software subscriptions do not imply four software licenses remain. Any cap belongs to the human setup service. An onboarding appointment date can create real timing information without a fake countdown on app access.

### Economics and guarantee

Monthly gross profit before acquisition is $20. Expected lifetime gross profit at an assumed eight months is $160. With $60 CAC and no other costs, gross-profit payback is three months; the business does not receive $160 on day one. Refunds, transaction fees, onboarding labor and churn variation may change this.

A clearly defined first-month satisfaction refund can be modeled for low-cost software, subject to actual storefront/payment terms. Guided human cleanup needs its own scope and remedy. Do not apply the same generous policy to two very different cost structures without calculation.

### Pilot and decision

Measure supported first-record completion, time to usable export, seven-day repeat use, support minutes, paid conversion and retention. A short activation test cannot validate eight-month retention. If activation is easy but repeat use is weak, redesign the routine or product experience before merely adding advertising. If human setup is the only way buyers succeed, price and staff it deliberately.

## 3. Deck construction: a costly-fulfillment contrast

### Hypothetical input

A builder quotes a $15,000 project with $11,000 direct materials/labor, $500 acquisition cost and a modeled $300 remedy allowance. Contribution before overhead/tax is $3,200. Site conditions, permits, weather, material availability and customer changes affect scope and schedule. None of these numbers are facts about Raven Rock Projects.

### Apply the methodology

The desired outcome is a usable outdoor space with a clear scope and process. Confidence may come from relevant completed-project evidence and a documented inspection/punch-list process. Reduce effort with a choices guide and consolidated approvals; reduce uncertainty with milestone updates. A sketch or design visit is early value, not a completed deck.

Candidate vehicles include unlimited custom design meetings, a guided choice package, a scoped site assessment and a written change-order process. Trim unlimited meetings; retain enough design review to avoid costly misunderstandings. A maintenance guide can be highlighted as a bonus, but necessary installation and required inspections cannot be optional extras.

### Guarantee tradeoff

A full refund after construction could leave the builder with materials/labor sunk, in addition to the refund. A workmanship-correction process tied to agreed specifications may better match controllable quality; its final terms need actual scope and applicable requirements. Do not guarantee all permits by a fixed date or all-weather completion without a feasible contractual basis.

A real crew schedule can support limited start slots. A supplier quote expiration supports a pricing deadline only if the quote and expiry exist. Omit those claims when unconfirmed. Do not carry a low-cost digital-course refund example into this business unchanged.

### Pilot decision

Test the clarity of scoped proposals and track conversion, change orders, delivery cost, milestone reliability and rework. A higher close rate paired with material overruns can be a worse offer. Ask which uncertainty is actually preventing the buyer from proceeding before adding bonuses.

## 4. Reusable practice worksheet

Complete these fields in an actual working document, using known facts and clearly marked assumptions. Do not make the user fill a long blank form if an interview is easier.

| Field | Completion standard |
|---|---|
| Buyer and trigger | Specific reachable person, situation and reason to act |
| Book concepts used | Chapter/PDF locator and why relevant |
| Desired result | Recognizable outcome within feasible scope |
| Obstacles | Journey-based list with evidence or assumption labels |
| Solution alternatives | Materially different vehicles for major obstacles |
| Trim decisions | Value/cost reason and preserved coverage |
| Final package | Components, owner, timing, inputs, exclusions |
| Price and economics | Consistent revenue/cash/cost units and sensitivity |
| Enhancers | Relevant bonus, chosen risk position, real limits if any |
| Name and message | Accurate wrapper matching actual delivery |
| Pilot | Buying evidence, fulfillment measures and next decision |

The output is a reasoned design ready for validation, not proof of market fit. Use the author's method to improve decisions; use actual buyer and delivery evidence to decide what works.
