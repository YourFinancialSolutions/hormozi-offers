# Scarcity and Urgency

Use scarcity and urgency only after the core offer is strong.

- **Scarcity:** a truthful limit on quantity, access, capacity, inventory, or availability.
- **Urgency:** a truthful limit based on time.

Both can increase action, but neither can create durable demand for a weak offer.

## 1. Select scarcity

Choose a limit the business can explain and enforce.

| Scarcity type | Appropriate use | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Total capacity cap | High-touch services or limited staff capacity. | Maximum active clients or units deliverable at promised quality. |
| Intake-rate cap | Business can serve many customers but only onboard a fixed number per period. | Real weekly or monthly onboarding capacity. |
| Cohort cap | Program, class, workshop, or implementation group. | Fixed group size and delivery schedule. |
| Limited product release | Physical inventory, edition, flavor, design, or batch. | Actual inventory or production decision. |
| Limited bonus | Bonus depends on inventory, seats, partner access, or personal time. | Actual quantity and allocation rule. |
| One-time availability | The item, event, or version will genuinely not return. | Clear operational or contractual reason. |
| Limited expert access | One-to-one advice, review, or intervention. | Calendar capacity and defined access boundary. |

When communicating capacity, state:

- The exact limit or remaining availability.
- What the limit applies to.
- The period or reopening rule.
- What happens after the limit is reached.

Maintain a waitlist when appropriate. Do not claim "sold out" unless it is true.

## 2. Select urgency

| Urgency type | Appropriate use | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cohort or start date | Delivery begins on a recurring schedule. | Actual start and cutoff dates. |
| Seasonal campaign | The promotion has a calendar-based wrapper or use case. | Published campaign dates and consistent terms. |
| Price-change deadline | Price will genuinely change. | Approved new price and effective date. |
| Bonus deadline | A bonus is removed or changed after the deadline. | Operational ability to enforce the change. |
| Opportunity decay | External economics, inventory, regulation, or timing makes later action less valuable. | Verifiable reason the opportunity deteriorates. |

Use a countdown only when it points to a real deadline. Once a deadline passes, honor it.

For year-round businesses, apply urgency to the promotion, price, bonus, onboarding date, or service level rather than falsely claiming the core service disappears.

## 3. Combine scarcity and urgency

Use both only when both are independently real.

Example structure:

`Enrollment closes [date] or when [real number] onboarding positions are filled, whichever occurs first. The next available start is [date or rule].`

Do not overload the offer with multiple weak timers and inventory claims. One clear limit is stronger than several suspicious ones.

## 4. Apply ethical controls

Before using a limit, answer:

1. Can the business prove it?
2. Will the business enforce it even when a late buyer offers money?
3. Is the buyer harmed by being rushed before receiving necessary disclosures?
4. Does the industry require cooling-off periods, filed rates, standardized terms, or special advertising disclosures?
5. Is the deadline long enough for an informed decision at this price and risk level?

Never:

- Reset a personal countdown after it expires.
- Invent limited inventory, demand, waitlists, capacity, or buyer activity.
- Create urgency around an unverifiable financial, medical, legal, tax, or insurance event.
- Withhold material terms until after the deadline.
- Use fear of loss to override suitability or informed consent.
